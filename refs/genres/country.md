# Country

Load when writing country, folk, or americana. A palette, not a checklist. Names
and specifics come from the person's own material, never from this file.

## What it is

A short story set to music. You're telling this to someone who wants to know
what happened. Specific people in specific places. The story comes first and the
feeling follows — you don't tell the listener how to feel, you show them the
moment and let it arrive.

The specific is the universal. "An '87 Silverado" reaches more people than "my
truck." The more particular the detail, the wider it lands.

## Voice

Conversational, nostalgic, earnest, working-class. A person talking on a porch,
not a poet performing. Conversational grammar is at home here — "me and him,"
"fixin' to" — when it's their real voice and not costume.

## What it does when it's working

- **Named people and places.** Not "a girl" but "Sarah from the Texaco."
- **Something happens.** Verse three isn't where verse one was.
- **Details that smell like somewhere real.** Cut grass, diesel, a screen door,
  gravel, coffee gone cold.
- **Show the moment, don't name the feeling.** "I sat in his truck alone" beats
  "I felt sad." The truck carries it better than the word does.
- **One small true detail as the key** — the time on the clock, the plate number
  — that unlocks the whole memory.

## Where it goes generic

These are places the autopilot phrase shows up. Don't grade them — ask what they
actually saw.

- Truck + beer + girl + Friday night, assembled instead of remembered.
- "Small town" with no specifics. Which town? Which road? Which person?
- Nostalgia with no actual memory under it.
- Dialect as costume. If it sounds like caricature, it's reaching.
- A story where nothing moves between verses.
- Naming the feeling instead of showing the scene that caused it.

## Structure, as a starting guess

Narrative verses that advance the story; a chorus that captures what it means; a
final verse that resolves or recasts it. A bridge can be direct address or a
time jump. Sensory specifics in the verses, the universal feeling in the chorus
— ground them, then lift them. Overrule this whenever the song wants something
else.
